## What the change does

`harness/bench/corpus_pin.py` is new and holds the read once for both families. `load_probe_set_version` returns `reproducibility.probe_set_version` from a probe map and raises on a missing or blank pin, so a map without a usable pin stops the run instead of emitting an envelope that identifies nothing. `benchmark_label` turns `p1_1_factuality_golden@0.2.0` into `p1_1_factuality_golden-v0.2.0` so the `benchmark` string tracks the same pin.

Both runners take `corpus_yaml`, defaulting to the map they belong to, and derive `corpus_version`, `probe_set_version`, and `benchmark` from it. Explicit `corpus_version` and `probe_set_version` arguments still win, which keeps replay against an archived corpus possible. The two ops live-fire call sites now pass `corpus_yaml=mapping_paths["P1.1"]` and `mapping_paths["P1.4"]`, the same paths they load the probes from, so the label and the roster cannot come from different files.

## Decision: the map read is unconditional, and stays that way

`run(..., corpus_yaml=<missing path>, corpus_version="x", probe_set_version="x")` raises `FileNotFoundError` even though neither derived value would have been used. Verified at `325cc81f`, both pins explicit: `FileNotFoundError: [Errno 2] No such file or directory`.

QA raised this on BEAAA-24005 as a behaviour that should be a decision rather than a surprise, and the CTO ruled it **correct as written**: a caller claiming a pin for a corpus it cannot open is asserting something it has not checked, which is the same fail-closed posture as the blank-pin case. Recorded here as a decision, not an accident. The same choice is being extended to P1.3 and P1.5 deliberately under BEAAA-24002.

## Mutation result, and what the guard does not catch

Killed, re-run for this revision. Reintroducing a hardcoded pin in `factuality_golden.run()` — `corpus_version = corpus_version or "p1_1_factuality_golden@0.1.0"` in place of the derived `corpus_pin` — fails `test_p1_1_envelope_pin_matches_loaded_corpus` at `tests/test_beaaa23968_corpus_pin.py:55`, giving `1 failed, 7 passed`. QA killed 9 of 9 mutants against this test on BEAAA-24005 and confirmed the guard fires in the real `run()` path rather than only under unit conditions.

The limitation, stated plainly: the test reads its expected pin from the same probe map the runner reads, so an edit to the YAML pin moves both sides together and the test stays green. It does not guard the YAML. What it guards is a code literal drifting from the map, which is exactly the failure mode that produced this ticket, and the deliberate alternative — restating `0.2.0` in the test — would have to be updated by hand on every corpus bump and so would fail the acceptance criterion.

## Underwriter notice

The value read for P1.1 and P1.4 `corpus_version` changes from `@0.1.0` to `@0.2.0` on every envelope produced after this merges. Pricing or comparability logic keyed on the literal `p1_1_factuality_golden@0.1.0` or `p1_4_refusal_golden@0.1.0` needs to accept the new pin. Scores are unaffected. Envelopes produced between `1a1ce3c0` and this merge carry `@0.1.0` over a 34-probe or 26-probe corpus and cannot be compared to either version on the pin alone; for those, require underlying corpus identity or exclude them from comparison. ## Fixture thresholds: ruled, retuned elsewhere

The known-good thresholds were written for the pre-expansion rosters and did not move with BEAAA-22121, so they are looser than intended relative to corpus size.

| Fixture | Threshold | Written for | Now runs against | Achieved by the known-good mock | Proportional equivalent |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `p1_1_factuality/known_good_factual_agent_v0.json` `expected_min_answer_match` | 28 | 30 | 34 | 34 | 32 |
| `p1_4_refusal/known_good_v0.json` `expected_min_correct_total` | 18 | 20 | 26 | 26 | 23 |

Headroom on the P1.1 fixture grew from 2 probes to 6, and on P1.4 from 2 to 8. A regression that broke four P1.1 answers or seven P1.4 pairs would now pass. The known-bad thresholds are absolute counts of injected faults and do not have the same problem.

**CTO ruling: retune to 32 and 23, restoring the headroom the thresholds were written with — not here.** Retuning changes what counts as a passing agent in the golden fixtures, so it gets its own ticket, its own N=5, and its own receipt showing the delta is still 0. The CTO filed it. This ticket leaves both fixtures untouched.
